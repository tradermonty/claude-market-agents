🚀 Microsoft's Record Day Reignites the AI Trade
Microsoft closed +15.51% at $451.10 after Wednesday night's fiscal Q4 report showed Azure revenue clearing $100 billion for the first time. The Wall Street Journal called it the "biggest one-day market-cap gain for any U.S. company"; Proactive noted it was MSFT's best session in nearly 18 years. The print pulled the entire AI-infrastructure complex higher — memory, storage, semicap and neocloud names all ripped — and lifted Technology +5.55% as a sector. The mirror image was Meta, which fell -7.95% on the same night's soft guidance and raised capex, dragging Communication Services to -2.26%. Barron's framed the split as "the Microsoft-Meta divide." Beneath the rally, Reuters flagged that the 30-year Treasury yield touched a 19-year peak.

🎯 Today's Key Points
  • Microsoft delivered the largest one-day market-cap gain in U.S. history: MSFT closed +15.51% at $451.10 on 110.0M shares (2.81x relative volume) after Azure revenue cleared $100B and EPS beat by 11.81%. Reuters, the WSJ and Barron's all led with it; Proactive called it Microsoft's best session in nearly 18 years. Wednesday's fear that AI capex was value-destroying flipped to validation in a single print.
  • The AI-infrastructure complex went vertical behind it: Lam Research +17.98% (best day since 1999, per Barron's), Sandisk +25.99% as the top S&P 500 gainer, Nebius +27.13%, Seagate +11.41% and Silicon Motion +21.66%. Memory and storage led because Microsoft's $190B capex commitment reads directly through to unit demand. Bloom Energy +26.49% rode a separate Mizuho upgrade, and the market's power-for-AI theme.
  • Apple beat and fell anyway — the day's most important after-hours signal: Fiscal Q3 sales topped $109B with iPhone revenue +22%, but services revenue missed and guidance came in light, sending the stock down ~5.9% after hours to $313.73. MarketWatch flagged it as potentially Apple's worst post-earnings drop in more than a decade. Management also warned that rising memory costs will pressure margins — the flip side of the very shortage lifting Sandisk and Seagate.
  • Amazon confirmed the cloud acceleration: AWS grew 37% year over year, the fastest in five years, revenue topped $200B and the backlog sits near $500B — good for +8.87% after hours. Read the headline EPS surprise (+215%) with care: a mark-to-market gain on Amazon's Anthropic stake does much of that work, and the company simultaneously nudged capex expectations higher on memory prices.
  • Breadth was better than Wednesday but still narrow: 114 names cleared the volume-surge screen with an average move of +10.5%, up from 74 names at +6.9% a day earlier, and uptrend names rose to 642. Even so, four of eleven sectors closed red and MarketWatch warned that the index rally "masks some underlying market weakness." IWM +1.39% lagged QQQ +3.30% by nearly two points.
  • M&A supplied the two largest non-AI movers: MarketAxess +29.45% on a $6B acquisition by Intercontinental Exchange — its biggest single-day gain in almost 18 years, on 14.08x relative volume — and micro-cap PN Smart Energy +120.80% after completing its Nanjing Cesun Power acquisition. Columbus McKinnon +41.45% was the cleanest fundamental move: record Q1 FY27 orders and sales, plus raised full-year guidance.
  • Bonds refused to join the party: TLT finished -0.06% while Reuters reported the 30-year Treasury yield touching a 19-year peak, one day after a hawkish-split Fed hold. Gold added +1.64%. Equities rallied on earnings, not on rates — a distinction that matters if Friday's month-end data disturbs the yield picture.
