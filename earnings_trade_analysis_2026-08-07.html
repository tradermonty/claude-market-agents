All 11 names reported yesterday (Thu 8/6) after the close — a compact, entirely earnings-driven cohort. Three B-grades lead the field (GMAB 71.0, HALO 70.0, ABNB 70.0), with zero A-grades: this is a selective, not a high-conviction, day. AIG is the lone D-grade, the only stock with a technically negative gap despite self-described "strong" results.

Gap size and fundamental surprise size frequently diverge. ROKU posted the single largest EPS surprise in the cohort (+78.39%) yet gapped just +0.29% — the stock was already up 6.99% into the print and sitting near 3-year highs, so the beat was largely priced in. RGA's +36.80% EPS surprise produced only a +0.56% gap. AFL and AIG both delivered in-line-to-modest beats and essentially flat gaps (+0.03% and -0.01%).

Volume participation is the cohort's weak link. 8 of 11 names show a 20-day average volume below their 60-day average (i.e. the pre-earnings advance came on shrinking interest). Only GMAB (104.6%) and RGA (94.9%, effectively neutral) avoid the <90% penalty band; ABNB and HALO sit just under the average-volume line at 93.9% and 91.0% respectively.

Trend structure is broadly healthy but uneven at the long end. All 11 names trade above their 50-day SMA, and 9 of 11 are comfortably above their 200-day SMA. The two exceptions — GMED (+0.94% vs MA200) and AIG (+3.13% vs MA200) — are only just reclaiming their long-term trend, which caps their MA200 scores at 3/5.

Important: the backtest score measures 2-10 day gap-continuation odds, not company quality. HALO (record revenue, guidance raise, RSI 78.77) and ABNB (guidance raised twice this year, AI-product narrative) both delivered the strongest fundamental prints of the day and still only score B, because their gap and volume buckets are mid-tier by the rubric's mechanics. Do not read a C or D grade as a bearish call on the underlying business.
