📝 Executive Summary
Major indexes closed mixed-to-higher on Tuesday, July 14, in a session dominated by the unofficial kickoff of Q2 bank earnings and a historic single-stock collapse in IBM. The June CPI report, released before the open, came in well below expectations — headline inflation fell a seasonally adjusted 0.4% month-over-month (consensus: -0.2%), pulling the annual rate down to 3.5% (consensus: 3.8%) from May's 4.2%, the biggest monthly decline since April 2020. Core CPI was flat on the month (2.6% year-over-year), also below consensus. Treasury yields fell sharply on the print, though traders still assign 63% odds to a Fed hike in September (down from over 75% a day earlier), reflecting the lingering inflation overhang from this year's Iran-linked oil shock.
Goldman Sachs (+9.00%) posted record quarterly profit on blowout trading and underwriting revenue (including fees tied to the SpaceX IPO) and hit an all-time high, while JPMorgan (+2.50%) and Bank of America (+1.88%) also beat estimates. Wells Fargo (-2.71%) and Citigroup (-5.29%) lagged. Those gains were nearly offset by IBM, which plunged 25.21% — its worst single-day decline since October 1987 — after warning that preliminary Q2 revenue and EPS fell short of Street estimates as enterprise clients shifted capex toward hardware ahead of expected price increases. The result: SPY +0.36%, QQQ +1.12% (Technology and chip-related AI infrastructure names led), DIA essentially flat at +0.04% (bank gains vs. IBM drag), and IWM +0.35%. TLT (+0.13%) and GLD (+1.37%) both firmed on the cooler inflation data and continued geopolitical risk premium.

📋 Today's Key Points
Broad Market Trend
A much cooler-than-expected June CPI report (headline -0.4% m/m vs. -0.2% consensus; 3.5% y/y vs. 3.8% consensus; core flat m/m, 2.6% y/y) drove Treasury yields sharply lower and reduced September Fed-hike odds to 63% from over 75% a day earlier. Equities were mixed-to-higher: SPY +0.36%, QQQ +1.12% (Technology led), DIA +0.04% (bank-earnings gains almost exactly offsetting IBM's historic plunge), and IWM +0.35%. Breadth remained solid at 703 confirmed uptrend stocks.
Hot Sectors
Technology (+1.87%) led on AI-infrastructure and chip-related strength (TSEM's Japan expansion, CLSK's data-center lease), followed by Basic Materials (+1.47%) and Communication Services (+1.18%). Healthcare (-1.60%) and Consumer Defensive (-1.14%) were the laggards in a classic risk-on, defensive-rotation-out session.
Notable Mover: Big Bank Earnings Kickoff
Goldman Sachs (+9.00%, all-time high) posted a record quarter on trading and investment-banking strength, including elevated equity-underwriting fees tied to the SpaceX IPO. JPMorgan (+2.50%) and Bank of America (+1.88%) also topped estimates, while Wells Fargo (-2.71%) and Citigroup (-5.29%, which "stood out for its loss" per market commentary) lagged — a genuinely mixed start to Q2 bank earnings season.
Notable Mover: IBM's Historic Plunge
IBM cratered 25.21% — its worst single-day decline since October 19, 1987 — after warning preliminary Q2 results fell short: adjusted EPS of $2.93 vs. $3.01 expected and revenue of $17.2B vs. $17.86B expected. CEO Arvind Krishna cited clients shifting capex toward hardware (servers, storage, memory) ahead of anticipated price increases; infrastructure revenue slid 7% while software still grew 5%. The full earnings call is scheduled for July 22.

Bonds, Gold & Volatility
TLT (+0.13%) and GLD (+1.37%) both firmed as Treasury yields fell on the cooler CPI print, with gold also catching a bid from the ongoing Iran-linked geopolitical risk premium in oil. VIXY (a VIX-futures ETF proxy) fell 1.71%, consistent with reduced near-term hedging demand on the benign inflation surprise.
Key Levels & Forward Look
SPY closed at $751.83, extending its advance. The rest of the week brings a heavy wave of large-cap bank and healthcare earnings — BlackRock, PNC, Progressive, Elevance, Cintas and M&T Bank on Wednesday; U.S. Bancorp, Abbott and Intuitive Surgical on Thursday; Travelers, Truist, Fifth Third and Regions Financial on Friday. Watch whether AEHR's strong after-hours reaction (record bookings, AI/EV test-equipment demand) carries into semiconductor-equipment sentiment, and monitor oil prices amid ongoing Iran-related tensions as the key upside risk to the inflation outlook.
